## Q: What is the difference between debt review and debt counselling?
A: They're essentially the same thing, and people use the two terms interchangeably. Debt counselling is the service a registered debt counsellor provides under the National Credit Act, and debt review is the formal process they run: they assess your finances and negotiate lower monthly instalments with your creditors, and you make one monthly payment to cover them. Voluntary surrender is a different, court-based route, which is why we ask. [DRAFT-VERIFY]

## Q: What is voluntary surrender of estate (VSE)?
A: It's a formal legal process under the Insolvency Act where, if you can't pay your debts, you apply to the High Court to surrender your estate. Your assets are handed to a trustee and sold for the benefit of your creditors, and once the process is completed you can get a fresh financial start. It has legal consequences, so a consultant first assesses whether it suits your situation. [DRAFT-VERIFY]

## Q: Is that the same as debt review?
A: No. Debt review keeps you paying your creditors through a restructured monthly payment. Voluntary surrender is a court process where your estate is surrendered to your creditors. Which one fits depends on your full financial picture, and the consultant will explain the options. [DRAFT-VERIFY]

## Q: Will I lose my house, car or other assets?
A: If you own a home, a trustee is appointed to look after your estate for your creditors, and your home would be sold, usually by auction, to pay off part of your debts. If you have a vehicle that's still under finance, the bank will take it back during the process. Some people still choose to go ahead once they understand that, and your consultant will explain exactly what it means for you before you decide anything. [From Craig, 3 Nov; DRAFT-VERIFY wording with David]

## Q: Can I go to prison because of my debt?
